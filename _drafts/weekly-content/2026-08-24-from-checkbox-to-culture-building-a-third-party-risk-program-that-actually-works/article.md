# From Checkbox to Culture: Building a Third-Party Risk Program That Actually Works

Third-party risk management often starts with a questionnaire. That is understandable, but a questionnaire is not a risk program. It is only one input into a broader operating model.

A practical program needs supplier inventory, criticality classification, ownership, verifiable evidence, issue tracking, and escalation. The useful shift is to treat TPRM as a business governance discipline rather than a documentation exercise.

The goal is not more paperwork. The goal is a live view of supplier dependencies, clear accountability, and better decisions about which external relationships deserve deeper control, monitoring, or risk acceptance.
