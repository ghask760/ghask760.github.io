Geopolitical Supply Chain Risks · October 2026

Recovery is not
availability

October’s signals point in different directions: stronger trade, recovering crude output, constrained fuel and new material projects. Resilience depends on whether the specific input, route and supplier needed by the business are available in time.

Cut-off: 15 October 2026 · 09:00 CEST Monthly intelligence 7 / 30 / 90 day actions
01 — Executive Snapshot

What changed since September

Monthly edition covering developments since the 14 September briefing; evidence cut-off: 15 October 2026, 09:00 CEST. This is an October-to-date assessment, not a full-month retrospective.

Analyst view

Treat diversification as a delivery capability. A different supplier name, a new project or a stronger macro forecast cannot replace a verified route, material qualification and available volume. Separate current evidence from forecasts and future capacity.

Changes and continuity

  • New: the October IEA and WTO outlooks show different recovery patterns.
  • New: the EU selects another group of strategic raw-material projects.
  • Carry-forward: UK Iran amendments are now beyond their effective date.
02 — Priority Signals

Evidence, implications and decisions

Critical Energy Maritime

More crude does not mean enough diesel

Confirmed fact. The IEA’s 14 October report estimates September oil supply rose by 2.5 million barrels per day to 102.3 million. Yet global diesel exports were roughly 700,000 barrels per day below a year earlier. It forecasts constrained middle-distillate supply well into 2027 and warns that renewed Gulf attacks threaten the recovery.

Analytical implication. Crude recovery and usable transport fuel are different signals. Test logistics continuity against delivered fuel availability, not only a headline oil price.

Owner
Procurement + Logistics
Evidence
Confirmed allocation, delivery route and stock coverage.

Recommended actions

  1. Obtain fuel-allocation and rerouting assumptions from critical carriers.
  2. Escalate when usable inventory cannot cover the replenishment lead time.
Primary source: IEA — Oil Market Report, 14 October 2026
High Trade Sector divergence

A stronger trade forecast can hide a weaker supplier

Confirmed fact. On 8 October, the WTO raised its 2026 merchandise-trade growth forecast to 3.9%, from 1.9%, while reducing its services-trade forecast to 3.3%, from 4.8%. AI-enabling goods accounted for 47% of merchandise-trade growth in the first half of 2026.

Analytical implication. These are forecasts and aggregate observations, not a health check for an individual vendor. Exposure can differ sharply between electronics capacity, transport services and energy-intensive production.

Decision trigger
A critical component’s confirmed lead time exceeds the recovery tolerance.
Evidence
Order-level commitments and qualified substitute capacity.

Recommended actions

  1. Split the watchlist by purchased category and operational dependency.
  2. Ask suppliers for actual lead-time, backlog and allocation changes before changing risk ratings.
Primary source: WTO — trade outlook, 8 October 2026
High Critical materials Capacity

A strategic project is not yet an available second source

Confirmed fact. The European Commission selected 46 additional strategic raw-material projects across 16 Member States on 9 October. They support the Critical Raw Materials Act’s 2030 capacity benchmarks for extraction, processing and recycling.

Analytical implication. Project selection is a diversification signal. Procurement still needs evidence of commissioning, product qualification and commercial volume before counting a project as an alternative supplier.

Decision trigger
An alternate exists on paper but cannot supply the required grade within tolerance.
Evidence
Qualification result, operating milestone and binding allocation.

Recommended actions

  1. Separate announced, financed, commissioned and customer-qualified capacity in the sourcing register.
  2. Track material grade, processor, contracted volume and earliest usable delivery date.
Primary source: European Commission — strategic projects, 9 October 2026
High Sanctions September follow-through

UK Iran changes have crossed their effective date

Confirmed effective-date follow-up. The UK’s Notice to Exporters 2026/18 describes expanded financial, trade and transport restrictions effective from 29 September. Covered areas include energy, maritime goods, petrochemicals and related technology and services.

Analytical implication. September’s preparation task is now an operational clearance task for transactions with a UK nexus. A supplier-name screen alone does not resolve product, service or transport restrictions.

Owner
Trade Compliance + Legal
Evidence
Dated transaction clearance with applicable licence conditions.

Recommended actions

  1. Have trade compliance confirm jurisdiction, goods classification, counterparties, transport and relevant licences before release.
  2. Reassess previously approved open orders where the transaction facts or applicable restrictions changed.
Primary source: UK Government — Notice to Exporters 2026/18

04 — Questions for Leadership

Make the next review measurable

Which alternate can actually deliver before our buffer runs out?

Ask for a named input, usable stock, replenishment time and a qualified substitute with confirmed volume. A substitution plan should include cash and working-capital requirements as well as technical approval.
