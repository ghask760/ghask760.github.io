Geopolitical Supply Chain Risks · November 2026

The truce is extended.
The dependency isn't.

China's January 2027 export-control reprieve, continuing sanctions and renewed navigation risk near Hormuz show why temporary relief should never be mistaken for resilient supply.

Cut-off: 15 November 2026 · CETMonth-to-date briefing7 / 30 / 90 day actions
01 — Executive Snapshot

The signal behind the headlines

This November-to-date edition covers information available by 15 November 2026, not a complete monthly retrospective. Facts are separated from editorial risk interpretation.

Analyst view

November offers a useful contradiction: controls on selected Chinese materials have been paused again, while supply chains remain concentrated and maritime vulnerabilities persist. A two-month reprieve should be used to prove alternatives, not to defer decisions. Sanctions and shipping developments reinforce the need to map legal entities, logistics routes and sub-tier inputs together.

What changed — and what did not

  • New: China's 9 November announcement pushes selected export-control suspensions to 10 January 2027.
  • New: the 11 November reported attack on navigation systems near the Strait of Hormuz; damage remains unverified.
  • Carry-forward: EU Russia sanctions and strategic-material concentration are continuing constraints, not new November packages.
02 — Priority Signals

Evidence, exposure and practical controls

CriticalChinaRare earths

Two more months of relief are not diversification

Confirmed reporting. On 9 November China announced a two-month extension of the suspension of selected export controls on rare earths, related equipment and technologies, industrial materials, and lithium battery/graphite items until 10 January 2027. Trade monitoring also records an extension of the pause in an additional 24% tariff on US imports, while a 10% layer remains.

TPRM interpretation. This is a temporary policy reprieve, not proof of assured supply. The biggest operational exposure lies below the Tier-1 supplier: magnets, refining know-how, specialty materials and licensing lead times.

Control question
Which components require Chinese-controlled materials or export authorisations?
Evidence to request
Tier-2/3 bill-of-material mapping, country of refining, buffer stocks, license status, qualified alternate specifications.

Recommended actions

  1. Create a January 2027 policy-expiry watchlist and owner.
  2. Map rare-earth magnets, graphite and battery material dependencies to actual SKUs and manufacturing sites.
  3. Ask suppliers for qualified alternative capacity and lead time rather than generic diversification statements.
Sources: Reuters, 9 Nov 2026 · ITC Market Access Map, 9 Nov 2026
CriticalMaritimeEnergy

Hormuz navigation attacks show that a recovered route can still be fragile

Confirmed reporting. Reuters reported on 11 November that Iranian attacks targeted shipping-navigation systems on Oman's Didamar island; the extent of damage was unverified. The report noted alternative navigation systems and that Gulf oil exports had recovered to more than 80% of pre-war levels in September–October through workarounds.

TPRM interpretation. Throughput recovery is not the same as predictable transit time, navigation resilience or insurance cost. Supplier continuity testing should include route outages and high-variance logistics, not just aggregate oil output.

Control question
Which supplier shipment or energy contract depends on this corridor and which alternative is operational?
Evidence to request
Shipment route maps, forwarder notices, insurance terms, lead-time variance, alternate port capacity and route switching thresholds.

Recommended actions

  1. Request current route/port dependencies from critical logistics and energy suppliers.
  2. Define time and cost thresholds for switching routing or building temporary inventory.
  3. Monitor verified maritime advisories; avoid assuming a full strait closure from the 11 November report.
Sources: Reuters, 11 Nov 2026
HighSanctionsRussia

Sanctions keep expanding beyond the immediate counterparty

Confirmed reporting. The EU's July 2026 21st Russia package extended restrictions across the shadow fleet, financial and crypto platforms, and dual-use supply chains. In October, the Council also renewed the separate restrictive-measures framework for Russian hybrid activities until October 2027.

TPRM interpretation. Screening only the contracted legal entity misses vessels, beneficial owners, transshipment routes, financial intermediaries and subcontractors. The October renewal is not a newly adopted November package.

Control question
Could a material shipment pass through a restricted vessel, intermediary or sanctioned refinery?
Evidence to request
Live screening logs, UBO data, shipping and payment counterparties, exceptions, legal review and re-screening triggers.

Recommended actions

  1. Re-screen critical suppliers, ownership chains and logistics intermediaries against current official lists.
  2. Apply event-driven checks after ownership, routing or payment-bank changes.
  3. Document escalation and blocked-transaction decisions with legal/compliance owners.
Sources: Council sanctions timeline, 2026 · European Council conclusions, Oct 2026
HighTrade policySnap-back

Trade compliance must have an expiry calendar

Confirmed reporting. The 9 November trade updates identify time-limited Chinese tariff and export-control suspensions extending into January 2027. These are distinct from permanently removed controls or unrestricted trade.

TPRM interpretation. Pricing, licensing and contract assumptions can change abruptly after an extension expires. Procurement teams need named decision triggers rather than relying on a headline about a trade truce.

Control question
What changes in unit cost and delivery if controls return in January?
Evidence to request
SKU-level tariff exposure, export licensing assumptions, quote validity dates, contract change-of-law clauses and alternative supplier readiness.

Recommended actions

  1. Record each temporary exemption's exact expiry and product scope.
  2. Model tariff and licensing snap-back on the most concentrated inputs.
  3. Agree which function authorises pre-buying, hedging or alternative sourcing.
Sources: ITC Market Access Map, 9 Nov 2026 · Reuters, 9 Nov 2026
HighConcentrationResilience

Strategic inputs need proof of substitution, not a second supplier name

Confirmed reporting. Recent trade and sanctions developments expose simultaneous dependencies on critical minerals, specialist logistics and restricted counterparties. These risks interact but the cited sources do not quantify an overall probability of disruption for any individual firm.

TPRM interpretation. The same sub-tier refiner or maritime route may sit behind multiple suppliers. A nominal dual-source strategy can conceal a single point of failure.

Control question
Are two named suppliers truly independent at the sub-tier and route level?
Evidence to request
Site-level dependency graph, material specifications, qualification results, confirmed alternate capacity, cash/working capital effect.

Recommended actions

  1. Identify common sub-tier materials and transport corridors in priority contracts.
  2. Test one substitute to a measurable switch-time and approved volume.
  3. Share residual concentration risks with procurement, finance and the board.
Sources: ITC Market Access Map · Council EU sanctions timeline

04 — Questions for Leadership

Make one dependency testable

Which of our alternatives could deliver before the temporary waiver expires?

Pick one high-impact material or lane. Document the originating plant, legal route, export permit dependency, usable buffer and a qualified substitute with a confirmed delivery promise. Treat a paper-based second source as an untested hypothesis.
