Risk Register

Building a Third-Party Risk Register That People Actually Use

November 2026·10 min read·Gabriel Hasik

Management Summary

A third-party risk register should be the operational memory of the supplier risk program. Too often, it becomes a spreadsheet that is updated before audits and ignored during decisions. The problem is not the idea of a register. The problem is designing it as a compliance artifact instead of a workflow tool.

A useful register helps answer practical questions: which suppliers matter, who owns them, what services they support, what evidence we have, what findings remain open, what decisions are pending, and what would happen if the supplier failed.

Core point: A risk register earns adoption when it helps people work, not when it merely stores information.

1. Start With Use Cases

Before adding fields, define the use cases. Procurement needs onboarding status. Business owners need supplier obligations and issues. Security needs evidence and findings. Management needs critical supplier exposure. Incident teams need contacts and impact. If the register does not serve these users, they will create their own parallel lists.

2. The Essential Fields

FieldWhy it matters
Supplier and serviceSeparates legal entity from actual dependency.
Business ownerCreates accountability.
Criticality tierDrives review depth and reporting.
Data and accessShows exposure.
Evidence statusShows assurance quality.
Open issuesConnects review findings to action.
Decision statusShows whether risk is approved, pending, or escalated.

3. Keep It Alive

A register goes stale when updates depend on annual cleanup. It stays useful when connected to events: new supplier, contract change, new service, access change, incident, expired evidence, open finding, or business owner change. Every event should update the supplier profile.

4. Avoid Data Hoarding

The register should not contain every possible attribute. More fields create maintenance debt. Start with the fields needed for decisions and reporting. Add fields only when they support a workflow, trigger, or management question.

5. Product Implication

The register is the natural core of a TPRM MVP. It connects suppliers, services, owners, evidence, issues, and decisions. If this object model is right, onboarding, review, monitoring, and reporting can build around it.

Practical next step: Design your register around the ten questions you most often need to answer during supplier reviews, incidents, and management reporting.
