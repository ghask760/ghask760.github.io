DORA & ICT Risk

What DORA Teaches Everyone About ICT Third-Party Risk

November 2026·10 min read·Gabriel Hasik

Management Summary

DORA applies to the financial sector, but its approach to ICT third-party risk is useful far beyond banks, insurers, payment institutions, and investment firms. It forces a discipline that many organizations need anyway: identify ICT dependencies, understand criticality, manage concentration, maintain information about contractual arrangements, and connect supplier risk to operational resilience.

The lesson is not that every company should copy DORA line by line. The lesson is that ICT third-party risk deserves structure because digital operations depend on external providers.

Core point: DORA offers a practical blueprint for treating ICT supplier risk as operational resilience risk.

1. Inventory Is Not Optional

DORA's Register of Information concept highlights a broader truth: organizations need a reliable view of ICT third-party arrangements. Without that, management cannot see dependencies, regulators cannot understand exposure, and incident teams cannot act quickly.

2. Criticality Drives Depth

DORA pays particular attention to services supporting critical or important functions. This principle travels well. Not every supplier requires the same review, but suppliers supporting important services deserve deeper governance, stronger contract clauses, better evidence, and clearer exit thinking.

DORA principleBroader lesson
ICT third-party registerKnow your technology dependencies.
Critical or important functionsPrioritize by business impact.
Concentration riskWatch shared providers and single points of failure.
Exit strategiesPlan for provider failure before it happens.

3. Concentration Risk Matters

Organizations often review suppliers one by one. DORA pushes a portfolio view. If many services depend on the same cloud, managed service provider, identity platform, or software vendor, the aggregate exposure may be larger than any single relationship suggests.

4. Product Implication

A TPRM product inspired by DORA should support both supplier-level and portfolio-level views. It should show which services rely on which providers, where concentration exists, which suppliers support important functions, and where exit options are weak.

Practical next step: Create a DORA-style ICT supplier register even if you are outside financial services. The exercise will reveal dependencies that ordinary vendor lists miss.
